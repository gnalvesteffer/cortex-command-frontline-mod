# Cortex Command Frontline Mod

An experimental solo auto-battler scenario for Cortex Command Community Project 7.0.0. Deploy batches of soldiers, buy research, and defend your brain against an AI opponent on a long battlefield.

## Installation

Copy `Frontline.rte` into the game's `Mods` directory and restart Cortex Command. In the activity selector, choose **Frontline Auto-Battler** and choose **Frontline Battlefield**, **Frontline Expanse Battlefield**, or **Frontline Highlands Battlefield**.

## Playing

Choose a difficulty in the game setup screen; it affects the AI's decision rate, while both sides use the same income rules. A two-step setup wizard opens before the battle. Set starting funds, base income, team unit cap (50 per side by default), outpost bonuses, and call-in availability, then review and click **Start Battle**. Both teams wait without income or deployments during setup. The 15-second grace period begins when the battle starts, and settings then lock.

Press **Tab** to open or close the panel. It locks the camera and uses the mouse while open. Left-click option controls to advance and right-click them to go back; purchase and action buttons still use left-click. On **Squad**, use the unit-page button to browse the roster and select counts and weapons. Squads begin with SMGs; research unlocks assault rifles, medikits, grenades, sidearms and unit armor. Each unit row can select a sidearm and packs of three medikits or three grenades; their prices are included per unit. Research unlocks the ARM checkbox for every unit type: it adds 50 health and costs 60 oz per unit. The researched miner has a fixed pistol and light digger, travels to the nearest detected gold deposit, and starts digging when it reaches the ore. Miners seek another deposit when a site is exhausted and wait if none remains. Use presets or save one custom batch for the match. Research unlocks the armored Gatling Drone and the missile launcher that counters armor. Select an order and target outpost on the Squad tab. Orders immediately update living troops and also apply to future deployments: Rush attacks the enemy brain, Advance moves to the selected post then resumes the attack, and Defend moves there and holds. Choose a captured outpost for a forward dropship delivery with a distance-based surcharge per unit and a 20-second deployment cooldown; base deployment has no surcharge and uses 10 seconds. The SPAWN selector shows the actual surcharge for its destination. Switching destinations cannot shorten an active cooldown. At the main base, squads deploy inside the bunker; a connected, two-level bunker approach provides overhead cover while leaving a route out. Passengers count toward the team cap while in transit; ships can be shot down, and surviving troops follow the current order after disembarking.

Grenade Packs research equips selected infantry with three Coalition Frag Grenades. The preset does not activate on release; its 4-second timed fuze starts when activated (normally as the native throw begins), so an unthrown grenade dropped on death remains unarmed. Frontline infantry use the native throw animation and targeting after a Frontline-only safety gate requires a visible, reachable enemy and clear friendly space around the blast area. Unsafe targets retain native firearm behavior when available, or native Sentry behavior when no firearm is available.

Forward pricing is symmetric for both teams: divide the distance between the two bases into one interval per outpost, measure the selected outpost's distance from your base, clamp it to that span, and round the number of intervals up. Each interval costs 25 oz per unit by default. The same price applies to player and AI squads, including every passenger.

The enemy AI scores the battlefield, gathers a six-unit force at a safe captured outpost, then advances the group together. Each persona has distinct research and support priorities; it selects owned safe posts for forward drops, falls back to a more affordable destination when needed, fills squads with unlocked units, and can spend its reserve on urgent defense. It adapts research to the player's observed focus and uses the same team commander, prices, cooldowns and limits as the player.

The AI can use unlocked, ready offensive support against clustered troops anywhere on the battlefield, favoring concentrations threatening friendly positions. It checks friendly-clearance, base-exclusion and jammer constraints, and also drops Cover at safe threatened off-post friendly squads using ground-probed positions; stray fragments can still be unsafe.

The small map has three outposts: West provides +5 income per second, Center reduces research costs by 20%, and East reduces call-in cooldowns by 20%. The two larger maps have five and nine posts, respectively, with repeating bonuses; all bonuses stack. Each additional research or cooldown post multiplies the remaining cost or cooldown by 0.8 (two posts give a 36% reduction), rounded up once. Highlands is 15,600 pixels wide and 1,600 pixels tall, with larger changes in elevation; its width stays below half the maximum texture size reported by the RTX 3090 used in development. Other GPUs can have lower limits. An uncontested troop captures a post over time; each is marked by a small outpost structure. On **Support**, choose an unlocked support, then click its target on the battlefield (press Tab to cancel). Each support is gated by its tech-tree research. Available call-ins include a Medic Drone dropship, cover, mines, a static turret, mining unit, Orbital Gatling Barrage, Orbital Precision Strike, Carpet Bomb, a support jammer and an Energy Shield. The jammer disrupts hostile call-ins aimed within its 180-pixel radius for up to 90 seconds, depending on research; it does not block weapons or bombs already falling. The Energy Shield blocks hostile projectiles inside its 180-pixel bubble for 30 seconds. The Orbital Gatling Barrage fires 72 fragmentation rounds over five seconds after a three-second warning; turret research adds 36 rounds. Carpet Bomb releases three bombs along its flight path. Cover, mine and turret dropships can be shot down in transit. These attacks can hit friendly troops.

Cover and Mines research each have three levels, delivering 3, 4 or 5 items per call-in. Cover research costs 280 / 560 / 840 oz and Mines costs 360 / 720 / 1080 oz, before outpost discounts. Mines requires Cover Lv.1; Turret requires Mines Lv.1. Their call-in costs and cooldowns remain unchanged as levels increase.

Research index 30 unlocks the Assault Squad: for 500 oz, a dropship delivers three Coalition Light soldiers with 200 current and maximum health on a 60-second base cooldown. The complete eligible pool is enumerated from loaded module HDFirearm presets and filtered for actual creation and positive gold value. Every spawnable non-stock firearm above the best stock value stays eligible, ranked by value and qualified name; rotating samples of three make the full pool reachable. If fewer than three qualify, the best stock firearms fill the remaining slots; the call-in is unavailable only if fewer than three spawnable firearms exist overall. Any position allowed by the existing support-target rules is valid.

The Research tab has Foundations, Arsenal, Support Network and Defense Network pages; use the page control to cycle them. The Defense Network diagram branches from Cover to Jammer and Mines, then joins Jammer Lv.2 and Turret at Energy Shield.

Automatic deployment starts enabled. Click **DEPLOY** or use **AUTO**; both share a cooldown and require funds and capacity for the whole batch. The closed-panel HUD shows deployment status, active outpost bonuses, each side’s death count, and elapsed match time. On stock engines it uses fixed screen text at the top center, avoiding camera jitter. Both brains are armed with Gatling Guns and defend their bunkers. Each team starts battle with one free HE turret in its upper bunker room. Gold deposits are buried across each map; miners can collect them, and proceeds enter the game funds counter. Training research improves unit perceptiveness. The Base tab offers brain armor, repairs and an HE-to-HE-Mk2 upgrade for the starting turret; destroyed turrets remain rebuildable. The AI can buy upgrades with the same prices and restrictions. Destroy the enemy brain to win. The match report displays deployments, team losses, captures, earned funds, enemy brain health lost and observed enemy troop health loss. Health loss is approximate and is not attributed to a particular attacker.

## Configuration

Edit the `CONFIG` table at the beginning of `Frontline.rte/Frontline.lua` to tune the roster, prices, income, bounty, unit limits, opening grace time, outpost capture and income, and call-in costs and cooldowns. `DEPLOY_COOLDOWN_MS` and `FORWARD_DEPLOY_COOLDOWN_MS` set the shared manual and automatic deployment intervals, defaulting to 10,000 and 20,000 ms. `unitSurcharges.forwardDeployment` sets the forward price per distance interval (25 oz by default).

The AI selects scored actions with a configurable softmax temperature
(`CONFIG.ai.temperature`, default 0.7): lower values favor the highest score,
while higher values increase strategic variety. Set an activity's
`aiTemperature` to override it; values at or below 0.001 select the highest
score deterministically. The AI also adapts its defensive priorities when the
player has made at least three deployments, at least three recent batches (in
the configured observation window) with the latest batch of four units or
fewer, no more than 0.3 research purchases per deployment, no call-ins, and
the match has passed 60 seconds.

## Status and testing

This is an experimental build. An earlier build loaded in Cortex Command 7.0.0, and the command panel and camera lock were verified interactively. The latest changes have passed Lua mock tests and the small scene has been started headlessly; manual targeting, mining effectiveness, balance, and complete matches still need interactive play-testing. Bounties currently count enemy deaths rather than verified kill attribution.

Run the isolated rules and HUD checks from the repository root with `luajit tests/test_frontline.lua`. These checks use mocks and do not replace in-game testing.

# Cortex Command Frontline Mod

An experimental solo auto-battler scenario for Cortex Command Community Project 7.0.0. Deploy batches of soldiers, buy research, and defend your brain against an AI opponent on a long battlefield.

## Installation

Copy `Frontline.rte` into the game's `Mods` directory and restart Cortex Command. In the activity selector, choose **Frontline Auto-Battler** and choose **Frontline Battlefield**, **Frontline Expanse Battlefield**, or **Frontline Highlands Battlefield**.

## Playing

Choose a difficulty in the game setup screen; it affects the AI's decision rate, while both sides use the same income rules. A two-step setup wizard opens before the battle. Set starting funds, base income, team unit cap (50 per side by default), outpost bonuses, and call-in availability, then review and click **Start Battle**. Both teams wait without income or deployments during setup. The 15-second grace period begins when the battle starts, and settings then lock.

Press **Tab** to open or close the panel. It locks the camera and uses the mouse while open. Left-click option controls to advance and right-click them to go back; purchase and action buttons still use left-click. On **Squad**, use the unit-page button to browse the roster and select counts and weapons. Squads begin with SMGs; research unlocks assault rifles, medikits, grenades, sidearms and unit armor. Each unit row can select a sidearm and packs of three medikits or three grenades; their prices are included per unit. Research unlocks the ARM checkbox for every unit type: it adds 50 health and costs 60 oz per unit. The researched miner has a fixed pistol and light digger, travels to the nearest detected gold deposit, and starts digging when it reaches the ore. Miners seek another deposit when a site is exhausted and wait if none remains. Use presets or save one custom batch for the match. Research unlocks the armored Gatling Drone and the missile launcher that counters armor. Select an order and target outpost on the Squad tab. Orders immediately update living troops and also apply to future deployments: Rush attacks the enemy brain, Advance moves to the selected post then resumes the attack, and Defend moves there and holds. Choose a captured outpost for a forward dropship delivery at an extra 25 oz per unit. Passengers count toward the team cap while in transit; ships can be shot down, and surviving troops follow the current order after disembarking.

The small map has three outposts: West provides +2 income per second, Center reduces research costs by 20%, and East reduces call-in cooldowns by 20%. The two larger maps have five and nine posts, respectively, with repeating bonuses; income bonuses stack. Highlands is 15,600 pixels wide and 1,600 pixels tall, with larger changes in elevation; its width stays below half the maximum texture size reported by the RTX 3090 used in development. Other GPUs can have lower limits. An uncontested troop captures a post over time; each is marked by a small outpost structure. On **Support**, choose a support, then click its target on the battlefield (press Tab to cancel). The panel stays closed after confirmation; press Tab to reopen it. Support cannot be targeted within 600 pixels of the opposing base; the same restriction applies to both teams. Available support includes: an artillery strike, medics, cover, mines, a dropped bomb, an orbital gatling barrage, or a support jammer. Place the jammer anywhere to disrupt enemy call-ins aimed within its 180-pixel radius for 20 seconds; its outline and countdown show the active area. Friendly support still works, and it does not stop weapons, grenades or bombs already falling. The orbital gatling fires 96 heavy rounds across the target area after a three-second warning; the upgrade adds 48 rounds. It can hit friendly troops. Support research increases cover and mine deliveries or extends the orbital burst.

Automatic deployment starts enabled. Click **DEPLOY** or use **AUTO**; both share a cooldown and require funds and capacity for the whole batch. The closed-panel HUD shows deployment status, active outpost bonuses, each side’s death count, and elapsed match time. On stock engines it uses fixed screen text at the top center, avoiding camera jitter. Both brains are armed with Gatling Guns and defend their bunkers. Gold deposits are buried across each map; miners can collect them, and proceeds enter the game funds counter. Training research improves unit perceptiveness. The Base tab offers brain armor, repairs and a rebuildable roof turret; the AI can buy these with the same prices and restrictions. Destroy the enemy brain to win. The match report displays deployments, team losses, captures, earned funds, enemy brain health lost and observed enemy troop health loss. Health loss is approximate and is not attributed to a particular attacker.

## Configuration

Edit the `CONFIG` table at the beginning of `Frontline.rte/Frontline.lua` to tune the roster, prices, income, bounty, unit limits, opening grace time, outpost capture and income, and call-in costs and cooldowns. `DEPLOY_COOLDOWN_MS` sets the shared manual and automatic deployment interval; its default is 10,000 ms.

## Status and testing

This is an experimental build. An earlier build loaded in Cortex Command 7.0.0, and the command panel and camera lock were verified interactively. The latest changes have passed Lua mock tests and the small scene has been started headlessly; manual targeting, mining effectiveness, balance, and complete matches still need interactive play-testing. Bounties currently count enemy deaths rather than verified kill attribution.

Run the isolated rules and HUD checks from the repository root with `luajit tests/test_frontline.lua`. These checks use mocks and do not replace in-game testing.
